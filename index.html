§ 01

Core Systems

Software for maritime logistics, running in the EU cloud or on your own servers. Open each row to see the problem it solves and how it works day to day.

No. System Domain Status
S-01 Container Terminal TOS Gate · Yard · Customs manifests Production

The problem

Trucks queue at the gate while staff re-type container numbers from the shipping line’s lists. One wrong digit and a container is lost in the system; when something goes wrong, nobody can say who moved what, and when.

What changes

  • The shipping line’s arrival notice is loaded in one click — no re-typing hundreds of numbers
  • Wrong container numbers are caught and listed, ready to send back to the agency
  • Every gate movement is tied to the correct customs manifest
  • Exact yard positions — block, bay, row, tier — and a full history per container

How it works day to day

Before the vessel arrives, the agency’s notice is loaded and checked. At the gate, the clerk confirms each arriving container; the system registers the movement against its manifest and records its yard position. A supervisor can see any container’s full history in seconds.

Security, in plain terms

  • No passwords to steal

    Staff can sign in with a passkey: the fingerprint or face unlock on their own phone or laptop. It only works on the real site, so a fake login page gets nothing.

  • Two-step sign-in, always

    Every administrator and operator confirms who they are with a second step. A stolen password on its own opens nothing.

  • One person, one session

    Signing in on another device ends the previous session. When someone leaves, their account is closed at once — never deleted, so their history stays on record.

  • A record that holds up in a dispute

    Every change and every refused attempt is recorded with who, when and from where, and archived month by month. When a container is disputed, damaged or part of an insurance claim, the answer is already on file.

For your IT team Next.js · MongoDB Atlas, EU region (Prisma) · passkeys (WebAuthn/FIDO2) + authenticator app with recovery codes · append-only audit trail with monthly archives · controls modelled on ISO 27001 Annex A

S-02 Reefer Vessel Stowage Planner Shipping agencies · Refrigerated cargo Production

The problem

Deciding where thousands of pallets go across a reefer vessel’s cooling sections takes hours of spreadsheets and experience. One mistake with temperature, or cargo for a later port blocking an earlier one, costs time and cargo.

What changes

  • Five complete stowage plans to compare, in minutes
  • One exact temperature per cooling zone; incompatible cargo never shares it
  • No overstow: each port’s cargo is reachable when the vessel gets there
  • Balance checked at every departure — the planner keeps the final word

How it works day to day

Exporters request space in their own portal. The planner turns bookings into a plan, adjusts it on a drawing of the vessel and emails the captain a PDF — no login needed over costly satellite links. Stevedores see what they need for loading.

Security, in plain terms

  • Each group sees only its part

    Agency staff, stevedores and exporters work in their own portals and see only what their role allows.

  • Checked on every action

    Permissions are checked every time something is saved, not only on the screen, and blocked attempts are recorded.

  • Critical changes ask again

    Administrators confirm their identity again before sensitive changes, and sessions end on their own after a working shift.

  • Passwords never stored in readable form

    Even with a copy of the database, nobody could read a password. Invitation and reset links are protected the same way.

For your IT team Next.js · MongoDB Atlas (AWS eu-north-1, Stockholm) · Python optimisation service (Google OR-Tools CP-SAT) · role checks on every server action · audit trail incl. denied attempts · PDF generation

CONCEPT PROPOSAL · LIMÓN, COSTA RICA

Puerto Limón: a citizen portal built around what people need to get done

An independent proposal to replace the current website of the Municipality of Limón, a port city of many cultures on Costa Rica’s Caribbean coast. Instead of mirroring the town hall’s org chart, the portal starts from the resident’s question, “What do you need to do today?”, and groups everything by task: permits and payments, reporting a problem in the street, doing business at the port, culture and history.

Unofficial proposal: not commissioned or approved by the Municipality of Limón.

Engineering Philosophy

01

Zero Bloat Architecture

No unnecessary software. Your system stays fast, inexpensive to run and easy to keep secure for years, and you never pay for features you don’t use.
